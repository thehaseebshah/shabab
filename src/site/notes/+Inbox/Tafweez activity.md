# Tafwid (Trusting Allah with Outcomes)

## Learning Objectives

- Distinguishing between human responsibility (*Asbab*) and Divine outcomes (*Nata'ij*), identifying anxiety traps caused by outcome fixation, internalizing servitude through *Haq-e-Azmat-e-Ilahi*, and applying practical emotional detachment during stressful situations.

---

## Activity 1 (The Two Domains: Asbab vs. Nata'ij)

### Data

| Field | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Age Limit | 12+ |
| Group Size | 3–6 players per team |
| Duration | 20 minutes |
| Prep Time | 2 minutes |
| Difficulty | Easy |
| Category | Indoor, Critical Thinking, Tarbiyah, Mental Models |
| Items Needed | Paper and pens (or whiteboard) for each team |
| Murabbi Rating | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ |

### Setup

Divide the room into teams. Give each team paper and a pen.

Write the 4 scenario options on the board (or assign one to each team):

1. **Scenario 1 (Studies / Exams):** Preparing for an important exam where you fear failing.
2. **Scenario 2 (Social Conflict):** Dealing with an upset friend or difficult relative who refuses to speak nicely.
3. **Scenario 3 (Family / Tarbiyah):** Trying to help a younger sibling who refuses to wake up for Fajr or study.
4. **Scenario 4 (Work / Livelihood):** Applying for jobs or trying to earn money in a competitive field.

### How to Play

1. **Table Breakdown (8 mins):** Each team draws a 3-column table on their paper:
   - **Column 1 — My Duty (*Asbab / Apne Karne Ka Kaam*):** Actions 100% within their control.
   - **Column 2 — Allah's Domain (*Nata'ij / Ghair-Ikhtiyari*):** Results completely outside their control.
   - **Column 3 — The Anxiety Trap (*Tadbeer Trap*):** What worry or 'what-if' usually ruins a person's peace in this situation?
2. **Rapid Presentations (8 mins):** One speaker from each team presents their 3 columns (1–2 minutes per team).
3. **Murabbi Feedback (4 mins):** Murabbi highlights if any team accidentally put an outcome under their duty (e.g. "making sure they agree with me") and corrects it.

### Sample Answers for Murabbi

- **Scenario 1 (Exams):**
  - *My Duty:* Study schedule, active recall, sleep on time, make Dua.
  - *Allah's Domain:* Exact test questions, grading curve, final ranking.
  - *Anxiety Trap:* Obsessing over "What if the paper is tough and my grade is ruined?"
- **Scenario 2 (Social Conflict):**
  - *My Duty:* Initiate Salam, speak with adab, offer handshake. *(Reference: Maulana Zakariyya in Kandhla)*.
  - *Allah's Domain:* The other person's mood, acceptance, or response.
  - *Anxiety Trap:* Demanding that they must apologize or smile back immediately.
- **Scenario 3 (Family / Fajr):**
  - *My Duty:* Polite, clear call (up to 3 times), encouraging tone.
  - *Allah's Domain:* Whether their heart softens and they actually get up.
  - *Anxiety Trap:* Getting into explosive anger because we try to force the outcome.
- **Scenario 4 (Work / Earning):**
  - *My Duty:* Learn the skill, build portfolio, send applications, maintain honesty.
  - *Allah's Domain:* Market shifts, company hiring decisions, exact income.
  - *Anxiety Trap:* Stalling out because "there are no jobs anyway."

### Murabbi Notes

- The key point to emphasize: **Misery happens when we neglect Column 1 and try to control Column 2.**
- *Tafwid* is not passive fatalism (*Tawaakul*). Doing Column 1 with excellence is a command of the Shariah.

## Activity 2 (The 3-Minute Tafwid Audit)

### Data

| Field | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Age Limit | 13+ |
| Group Size | Individual journaling followed by brief debrief |
| Duration | 15–20 minutes |
| Prep Time | 2 minutes |
| Difficulty | Easy |
| Category | Indoor, Personal Reflection, Emotional Resilience, Tarbiyah |
| Items Needed | Notebooks / sheets of paper, pens |
| Murabbi Rating | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ |

### Setup

Ensure Shabab have a quiet environment and individual paper. Emphasize that these sheets are personal and will **not** be collected or read aloud.

### How to Play

1. **Silent Writing (6 mins):** Shabab fold their paper into two halves:
   - **Left Side (The Worry & The Outcome):** Write 1 or 2 current problems causing stress (e.g., school pressure, friendship issues, future uncertainty) and what specific outcome they are dreading or demanding.
   - **Right Side (The Immediate Action):** Write the single concrete, ethical action Allah expects them to take right now regarding this problem.
2. **The Surrender Line (2 mins):** Participants take their pen and cross out the Left Side's outcome demand, resolving silently:
   > *"I will focus on doing the right side. The left side is in Allah's hands."*
3. **Closing Discussion (7 mins):** Murabbi invites general, voluntary reflections (without revealing private details) using the prompts below.

### Key Discussion Points for Murabbi

- **Haq-e-Azmat-e-Ilahi (The Right of Allah's Majesty):** We surrender to Allah not just as a stress-relief technique, but because He is the Master (*Aqa*) and we are His servants (*Ghulam*). Peace of mind (*Sukoon*) is a natural byproduct.
- **Heart of Ice (*Barf Sa Dil*):** Remaining calm when results do not go our way, knowing that our reward was in the effort, not the outcome.
- **Talween to Tamkeen:** It is normal to feel anxious again after 10 minutes. Every time the anxious thought returns, redirect attention back to your immediate duty.

---

## Murabbi Debrief Prompts

1. *"Why does obsessing over outcomes make a person angry or paralyzed?"*
2. *"When Maulana Zakariyya visited his hostile relative who turned his face away, why was he completely unbothered?"*
3. *"What is the difference between being lazy (fatalism) and practicing genuine Tafwid?"*
